I study how the contexts in which people interact, online and offline, shape who they connect with. My work draws on large-scale surveys and digital trace data, often combining both, and has developed through collaborations with several research groups and data infrastructures.

## Homophily in population-level social networks

My PhD dissertation, *From Followers to Close Ties: Homophily in Population-Level Social Networks* (Northeastern University, 2026), studies homophily and tie similarity across different kinds of relationships and contexts. Each chapter uses data from a different project I took part in:

- **Online attention networks.** Using the follower ties among accounts of the Lazer Lab's Twitter panel, I show that geographic proximity matters more than demographics and partisanship in who follows whom online, and that age and race homophily outweigh partisan homophily. Published as a book with Cambridge University Press: [*Who Follows Whom?*](/publications/#who-follows-whom).
- **Racial segregation in close ties.** Using ego-network data from the COVID States Project (more than 57,000 strong ties), I show that the racial composition of where people live shapes, but far from fully explains, racial segregation in close relationships. [Manuscript in preparation](/publications/#wip-race-homophily).
- **Similarity by tie strength.** Using PATCHWORK's social network survey, I study how the similarity between people varies with the strength of their relationship, from acquaintances to close ties, in four European countries. [Manuscript in preparation](/publications/#wip-tie-strength), as a joint PATCHWORK paper.

## The COVID States Project / CHIP50

The [COVID States Project](https://www.covidstates.org/), now the [Civic Health and Institutions Project (CHIP50)](https://www.chip50.org/), is a multi-university consortium that has surveyed more than 20,000 respondents several times a year across all US states since 2020. As part of the core data team, I worked across the data pipeline: questionnaire design, survey weighting, data-processing code, and the tables and figures for public reports and dashboards. I co-authored about 50 public reports.

This work led to my first-authored study on the accuracy of large opt-in, non-probability surveys for estimating COVID-19 infection and vaccination rates at the state level ([JSSAM 2026](/publications/#jssam)). For a study with Mauricio Santillana's group on behavioral change during the pandemic, I produced the state-level survey time series and wrote the description of the data ([PNAS 2025](/publications/#pnas)). Other team publications cover:

- **Trust in science:** [Nature Human Behaviour 2026](/publications/#nhb-trust), [Public Opinion Quarterly 2025](/publications/#poq-trust).
- **Public health:** long COVID ([JAMA Netw. Open 2022](/publications/#jama-longcovid)), COVID-19 tracking with at-home tests ([JAMA Netw. Open 2024](/publications/#jama-tests)), mobility and depression ([JAMA Netw. Open 2023](/publications/#jama-mobility)), social media use and depression ([JAMA Netw. Open 2021](/publications/#jama-socialmedia)), firearm ownership and depression ([JAMA Netw. Open 2022](/publications/#jama-firearms)), social isolation in older adults ([J. Affective Disorders 2023](/publications/#jad)), and resilience in depression ([Depression and Anxiety 2021](/publications/#depression-anxiety)).
- **COVID-19 misperceptions:** [Group Processes & Intergroup Relations 2021](/publications/#gpir).

The ego-network data collected by the project are the basis of the second chapter of my dissertation, and of [ongoing work on social support](/publications/#wip-social-support) during and after the pandemic.

## Online information ecosystems

Much of my work at the Lazer Lab uses one of its main data infrastructures: a large panel of Twitter accounts matched to US voter records, which links online behavior to demographic, political and geographic information. With these data I have contributed to studies on:

- who follows whom online, the first chapter of my dissertation ([Cambridge University Press 2026](/publications/#who-follows-whom));
- the websites shared by different demographic groups, released as an open dataset ([DomainDemo, Scientific Data 2025](/publications/#domaindemo));
- the decline in information quality on X after the change in ownership of Twitter ([HKS Misinformation Review 2025](/publications/#hks));
- the challenges of measuring gender online ([Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication 2024](/publications/#jcmc-gender));
- how stance expressed on social media aligns with public opinion surveys ([EMNLP 2021](/publications/#emnlp)).

I also contributed to the [National Internet Observatory](https://nationalinternetobservatory.org/), an NSF-funded infrastructure at Northeastern University that collects survey and online behavioral data from consenting participants for research, and presented it at an [invited workshop in Manchester](https://digisurvor.github.io/workshop_1/) in 2025 ([slides](https://digisurvor.github.io/workshop_1/docs/NIO_Manchester.pdf)).

## PATCHWORK: a network science approach to social cohesion

[PATCHWORK](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101020038) is an ERC Advanced Grant project led by Miranda Lubbers at the [COALESCE Lab](https://coalesce-lab.com/) (Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona). It studies social cohesion in European societies through the networks of relationships that connect, or separate, social groups, using a large cross-national survey. I collaborated with the project as a visiting researcher from 2022 to 2026 and contributed to the design of its survey, which collects detailed data on people's acquaintanceship and personal networks. I use these data in the third chapter of my dissertation, on how similarity varies with tie strength ([manuscript in preparation](/publications/#wip-tie-strength)).
